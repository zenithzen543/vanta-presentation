PANEL QUESTIONS — FROM THE DECK

SLIDE 1 — TITLE
Q. What are you presenting?
A. A live heirloom-seed catalog — Thorn & Furrow — on Next.js and Sanity. Product, content model, and the publish loop. I can open this catalog. Two other live sites come after the close; I will not open their studios.

Q. What do Next.js, Sanity, and TypeScript do here?
A. Next.js App Router is the public site. Sanity is the CMS — packets and homepage sections are documents. TypeScript is how the types stay honest between Studio fields and the pages. Production deploys on Vercel, but this slide is the product stack, not a hosting lecture.

Q. What is “product, content model, publish loop”?
A. The product is the catalog visitors see. The content model is five documents so Icehouse Tomato is one record, not three pastes. The publish loop is: editor publishes in Studio, Next.js pulls the page, the storefront updates without a code deploy.

Q. Is this a SaaS?
A. No. Nobody logs in. There is no account or billing. It is a public marketing site for a farm that sells seed. Success is a packet-list request — a lead — not a signup.

SLIDE 2 — TYPES VS CATALOG
Q. What do you own versus what marketing owns?
A. I own the types — which fields exist, how pages compose, how publish and tracking work. They own the weekly catalog: headings, stock, stories. A sold-out packet is a field, not a deploy.

Q. Why is the season a document, not a deploy?
A. Catalog No. 14 and Spring 2026 live in the CMS. Marketing can change season copy in Studio. Engineering does not ship a new build for a new heading or a wait-list packet.

Q. Why seventeen packets? Why Tivoli / zone 6a?
A. If a variety is listed, they grow it — no ornamental fillers. They pack March through June, the week a packet ships, for zone 6a on River Road in Tivoli. That is why the catalog is editorial, not a generic product grid.

Q. Is this live?
A. Yes. The public catalog is on Vercel at the URL on the slide. Locally it is also localhost:3000 with Studio at /studio.

SLIDE 3 — WHAT VISITORS SEE
Q. What are the four public surfaces?
A. Homepage at / — ordered CMS sections: masthead, featured packet, sowing table, spring list. Catalog at /varieties — seventeen packets grouped by family. Journal at /journal — seasonal field notes, not a blog calendar. Mail order at /order — a letter, not a cart.

Q. Why isn’t Studio on the storefront?
A. Studio lives at /studio for editors. It is not linked on the public site. The storefront should feel like a live catalog, not a CMS demo.

Q. Why no shopping cart?
A. Visitors mark packets, tell us their zone, we write back, then pack the week it ships. That is a qualified lead. A fake checkout would pretend this is ecommerce it is not.

SLIDE 4 — WHY THIS SHAPE
Q. Why not a 2×3 feature grid / SaaS homepage?
A. That layout is a product-marketing homework site. A catalog needs a masthead, a featured item, a table, and an index — forms marketing actually uses for editorial products.

Q. What is a document here?
A. Icehouse Tomato is one Sanity record. Home, the index, the packet page, and the order form all reference it. Change stock once — every surface updates.

Q. What is composition?
A. The homepage is an ordered section array. Editors reorder masthead, featured packet, sowing table, spring list without a deploy. I own the section types; they own the order and the copy.

Q. How does tracking survive a copy change?
A. GTM keys off eventId — hero_order on the homepage CTA, nav_order in the header — not the words on the button. Marketing can rewrite “Request a packet list.” Measurement does not break.

SLIDE 5 — EDITOR TO PRODUCTION
Q. Walk from editor to live page.
A. An editor publishes in Sanity Studio. Next.js pulls with GROQ. Pages are React Server Components. Production uses ISR about every sixty seconds, plus a signed revalidate API after publish so we do not always wait a minute. The same heading is then on /.

Q. What is GROQ?
A. Sanity’s query language. How Next.js asks for documents. It is not Groq the AI company. Queries live with the site, not in the browser on every view.

Q. What is ISR? Why also a webhook?
A. Incremental Static Regeneration: the catalog is cached and refreshes on a timer, about sixty seconds. After publish, a signed POST to /api/revalidate drops cache immediately so a new heading can show before the timer.

Q. What if Sanity is down?
A. A local seed still renders the catalog. The demo does not go blank. Photographs fall back to a local file per slug if the asset is missing.

Q. What is a React Server Component?
A. The page runs on the server, fetches there, then the visitor gets HTML. We do not do a client-side CMS call on every view.

SLIDE 6 — FIVE DOCUMENTS
Q. Name the five documents.
A. Farm settings — name, nav, primary CTA, footer, default SEO. Catalog homepage — SEO plus an ordered section array. Variety — the product: latin name, family, days, packet, stock, photo, story. Journal note — season, excerpt, body. Workshop — date, place, seats. Same desk, labeled The farm.

Q. Why not duplicate Icehouse on three pages?
A. One variety document. Home, packet page, and order form reference it. Change common name, latin, or slug once; every surface follows.

SLIDE 7 — LIVE DEMO (they may ask how it works, not how to perform it)
Q. What does the live demo prove?
A. The masthead is a section. The featured packet is a document, not a widget. Icehouse on /varieties/icehouse-tomato is the same record. Publish in Studio on Catalog homepage masthead, reload /, the heading is live — no git push. The CTA label is copy; eventId is the contract.

Q. How does an editor change the homepage heading?
A. Studio, desk The farm, Catalog homepage, Catalog sections, Catalog masthead, Heading, Publish. Reload the public site. Draft is not live. That is publish, not deploy.

Q. How do they change a packet?
A. Varieties, open Icehouse Tomato, edit Stock, Packet, or Story, Publish. Stock is In the shed, Limited, or Wait list. One document updates home, packet page, and order form.

SLIDE 8 — WHAT I WOULD DEFEND
Q. Why RSC and GROQ instead of fetching Sanity in the browser?
A. Pages fetch on the server. Faster, cacheable, no CMS keys in the client on every view.

Q. Why references?
A. One variety document shared across surfaces. Duplication is how catalogs rot.

Q. Why event IDs?
A. GTM listens for hero_order and nav_order. A rewrite of the button does not break measurement.

Q. Why ISR plus a webhook?
A. Fast pages, still fresh. Timer for the common case; signed revalidate after publish when marketing needs it now.

Q. Why no cart?
A. A qualified packet-list request is closer to a real marketing funnel than a fake checkout. Mark packets, zone, we write back, pack the week it ships.

SLIDE 9 — CLOSE
Q. What does the close line mean?
A. The catalog is the product visitors use. The CMS is how headings, stock, and stories change without engineering shipping a new build.

SLIDES 10–15 — NDA / JAMB / SLINGSHOT
Q. Can you open Jamb or Slingshot Studio?
A. No. NDA. I can name the public business and which surfaces Sanity feeds. Thorn & Furrow is the walkthrough.

Q. Why talk about them like an ads business?
A. Same three questions: who buys, what is inventory, how a visit becomes a sale. Then where Sanity sits. I will not name schemas or fields.

Q. Who is Jamb’s buyer? What do they sell? How does it sell?
A. Interior designers, collectors, country-house clients. Flagship Pimlico Road; showrooms in Los Angeles, Chicago, Dallas, Atlanta, Palm Beach. Unique antiques — chimneypieces, lighting, furniture — and handmade reproductions from the south London workshop so a design stays available after the antique sells. High-ticket, consultative. The site is the catalog for the showroom. Enquiry, not a cart. Sanity owns collections and journal stories. Next.js is the storefront.

Q. What are Jamb’s four public collections?
A. Fireplaces — antique chimneypieces and stone or marble reproductions, bespoke when the room needs a scale not in stock. Lighting — hanging globes, lanterns, wall lights; the Original Globe started the reproduction line. Furniture — English country-house seating and tables, antique and made. Journal — stories for architects and designers, not a blog calendar.

Q. What problem does Slingshot solve?
A. Flow cytometry labs: biopharma, CROs, academic cores, cell therapy, instrument makers. Donor cells expire and drift lot to lot. Polystyrene beads have the wrong scatter. The control is the weak point in the assay.

Q. What do they sell, and how does a visit become a sale?
A. Shelf-stable synthetic cell mimics — polymer particles that scatter, fluoresce, and stain like real cells. Catalog SKUs plus custom. A scientist finds a control, reads a protocol, orders a reagent. Repeat purchases. Sanity owns product and resource documents.

Q. What are Slingshot’s public surfaces?
A. Homepage — catalog of controls: compensation, unmixing, viability, immunophenotyping. How Mimics Work — not a biologic, not a bead; the page that sells the category. Shop — SKUs by purpose, then a cart; custom on request.

Q. Slingshot has a cart. This catalog does not. Why?
A. Different sale. Labs order reagents as repeat SKUs. Thorn & Furrow packs seed by letter for a season. Each site’s conversion matches the business. I still cannot open Slingshot’s desk.

Q. Why a seed catalog instead of cloning a trust/compliance homepage?
A. Cloning someone else’s marketing site is not the work. This still has a homepage composer, references, ISR, Studio UX, and GTM IDs. You can judge the modeling, not a photocopy.

THORN & FURROW — MODELING
Q. What is in a Variety document?
A. Common name, latin name, slug, catalog family, days to maturity, sowing, packet price, flavor, isolation, kitchen use, stock, photograph, story. That is the product object.

Q. What homepage section types exist?
A. Catalog masthead, featured variety, sowing table, variety index, journal strip, workshop list, letter/CTA. Unknown types are skipped. Editors do not type raw HTML.

Q. How do you keep editors from breaking the page?
A. Typed sections, required fields, no raw HTML. If a section type is unknown, the UI skips it.

Q. Webflow to Sanity — how would you migrate?
A. Map collections first: varieties, journal, workshops. Redirects and titles travel with documents, not with a page tree.

Q. Where does the code live if we ask to see it?
A. Types in sanity/schemaTypes. Desk in sanity/structure.ts. GROQ in sanity/lib/queries.ts. Home page is an App Router server component. Revalidate is app/api/revalidate/route.ts. Seed fallback is lib/content/seed.ts. I will not show Jamb or Slingshot code.
